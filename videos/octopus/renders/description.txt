TITLE: What if you had an octopus's arms?

Do octopus arms have their own brains?

In a way, yes. About two thirds of an octopus's brain cells are not in its head but in its arms. Each arm has its own cluster of nerve cells, so it can feel, reach and grab with little help from the brain.

The suckers do more than hold on. They are packed with sensors that pick up chemicals, so an octopus tastes whatever it touches.

So why doesn't an octopus tangle itself up? Scientists found that its skin carries a chemical that stops its own suckers from grabbing on. Your arms take every order from your one brain, which is the reason you can tie your shoes without a fight.
