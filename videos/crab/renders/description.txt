TITLE
What if you had a crab's claws?

DESCRIPTION
How does a crab pull its claws out when it molts?

The coconut crab, the biggest crab that lives on land, has the strongest pinch ever measured in a crustacean. In one study the strongest crab pinched with 1,765 newtons, about 180 kilos of force. That is how it gets into coconuts.

But a claw is hard shell, and shell can't grow. To get bigger, a crab has to molt. First the big muscle inside each claw shrinks, by about half, so it can be pulled back out through the narrow joints. Then the crab climbs out of its old shell, and the new claws come out soft.

A coconut crab hides in a burrow for weeks while its new shell hardens, and it eats its old shell to get the calcium back.

A coconut crab's pinch can badly hurt a person, so leave wild ones alone.
