TITLE
What if you had a fish's gills?

DESCRIPTION
Could you breathe underwater with gills?

He got fish gills, and nearly drowned in the sea. Water holds about thirty times less oxygen than air, so gills have to push a lot of water past them to get enough.

A fish can do it because it is cold-blooded and needs only a small fraction of the oxygen a warm-blooded body burns. Its gills pull out about 80 % of the oxygen in the water. Even with gills that good, a resting person would need about 50 liters of water a minute, five buckets, and around forty while swimming.

Out of the water it gets worse: without water holding them apart, gill filaments collapse and stick together, so a fish suffocates in air even though air is full of oxygen.
