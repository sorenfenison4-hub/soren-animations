TITLE
What if you were as big as a blue whale?

DESCRIPTION
Why can't giants exist?

A blue whale is about 30 meters long, the biggest animal that has ever lived. To be that tall you would have to grow about 17 times taller. But your weight grows in every direction at once, 17 x 17 x 17, so you would be about 5,000 times heavier: around 350 tonnes, more than the heaviest blue whale ever weighed.

Your bones would not keep up. A bone's strength depends on how thick it is across, and that only grows 17 x 17, so your bones would be about 300 times stronger while carrying 5,000 times the weight. Galileo worked this out almost 400 years ago: a giant built exactly like a person would be too heavy for its own bones.

A blue whale gets away with its size because the sea holds it up. When a whale is stranded on a beach, its own weight presses down on its chest and organs, which is one reason stranding is so dangerous for whales.

If you ever find a stranded whale, keep back and call your local marine rescue or wildlife service.
