Title: What if you were as tough as a tardigrade?

Can tardigrades really survive anything?

Tardigrades, also called water bears, are about half a millimetre long and famous for surviving things that would kill almost any other animal. The catch: they only do it dried out. When their home dries up, they curl into a tiny barrel called a tun, lose about 95 percent of their water, and their body nearly stops, running at around 0.01 percent of normal.

Dried out like that, tardigrades survived 10 days in open space on a satellite in 2007, and a tardigrade from an Antarctic moss sample woke up after 30 years in a freezer at minus 20 degrees and later laid eggs. Add water, and they come back to life.

Awake, they are surprisingly fragile. In a 2020 study, half of the active tardigrades died after one day at 37 °C, about the temperature inside your body.
