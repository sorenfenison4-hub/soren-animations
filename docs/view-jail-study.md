# View-jail study (2026-11-17)

## What "view jail" is
Not a penalty, nothing to appeal. YouTube shows every Short to a small test audience first (a few hundred to
~2,000 people) and compares how they react - watched vs. swiped away, how much they watched, replays - with similar
Shorts. Beat them and it is pushed wider; lose and it stalls. YouTube publishes no threshold; "1K jail" is a creator
term. A Short stuck at 1-3K lost its test; the channel is not punished.

## Findings
1. Like rate is the same everywhere (owl 1.2 %, others 0.5-1.6 %): people who see the videos like them equally.
   The owl won the feed test (fewer swipes, more watch time / replays), not on quality.
2. Premise: the owl's head turn is a famous trick people already wonder about, and the first line takes away
   something viewers are using right now ("you'd never move them again") - they test it while watching. Woodpecker
   brains, frog eyes swallowing, gecko hairs and mantis shrimp are less famous.
3. First of its kind, then 9 near-copies in 3 days (4 on 11-16, two 1 h apart; 3 of them about eyes again). Test
   viewers who had seen the owl got repeats, and the uploads competed with each other.
4. Two changes from 11-16 the owl never had:
   - Frame-0 text became a lowercase comment bubble repeating the title, instead of a claim ("HIS EYES ARE STUCK").
   - The voice ends on "X or Y? Comment below." instead of a story beat that runs straight into frame 0 ("So he turns
     the slow way."). A spoken "the end" right before the loop invites a swipe instead of a replay - and it brought
     almost no comments (chameleon 0, frog 0, woodpecker 2).
5. Luck: Shorts reach is very uneven; one breakout in ~12 uploads is normal at this size.

## Rules adopted (the user, 2026-11-17) - now in CLAUDE.md
- Claim HookTitle at frame 0 again (no comment-bubble opener).
- The voice ends on the story beat that loops into frame 0; the comment ask is on screen only (CommentAsk, either/or).
- 1 Short a day, spaced; no repeat body part within 3 videos.
- Only famous, "wait, really?" animal tricks (screen the premise with the owl test first).
