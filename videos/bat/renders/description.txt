TITLE: What if you had a bat's ears?

How do bats not go deaf from their own screams?

Bats find their way and catch insects in the dark by screaming and listening for the echoes. Some of them scream as loud as a jet engine, measured right in front of the mouth, and when a bat closes in on a moth it can call up to 190 times a second.

So why don't they deafen themselves? A tiny muscle in the middle ear tightens just before each call and dulls the sound reaching the inner ear, then relaxes in time for the bat to hear the faint echo.

Your ears have no system like that. A sound that loud right next to your ear can damage the hair cells that let you hear, and those don't grow back. Protect your ears around very loud noise.
