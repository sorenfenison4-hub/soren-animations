Title:
What if you had a polar bear's fur?

Description:
Can polar bears overheat?

Yes. A polar bear's coat is built to keep heat in. Its hairs are clear and hollow, and they only look white because they scatter light. The skin underneath is black, and under the skin there is a layer of fat up to 10 centimeters thick.

It works so well that the bear struggles to get rid of heat. When scientists walked polar bears on a treadmill, their body temperature climbed faster the faster they walked, so polar bears mostly move slowly and rest often.

With that coat on you, one jog would push your body past 40 °C (104 °F). That is heat stroke.

Heat stroke is an emergency: if someone is overheated and confused, get them cool and call for help.
