## What the frames show

- SciFX bite vs leg: one fixed test rig (bite-force machine, a big digital number, a fake human leg). A new animal
  every few seconds bites the leg, the number climbs (the bear shows 1,160 psi), the last one (crocodile) wrecks the leg.
  Same frame all the way through, readable with the sound off and in any language.
- Tior: real footage, one animal per beat with a one- or two-word label (GECKO / CLIMB ANYWHERE, AXOLOTL, IMMORTAL),
  each one more extreme than the last.
- CoolFacts: photoreal animals with cartoon faces, word-by-word captions, a different power every scene.
- Wrong superpower: live action, the face fills the frame, the power backfires as one quick gag (stuck in his own web).
- Ours (owl): the guy and the owl small and full-body under a text box. Every outlier above has its subject filling
  half the frame or more.

## Conclusions

1. In this niche the small-channel million-view Shorts share one format: ONE QUESTION, MANY ANIMALS, ONE NUMBER,
   ESCALATING to the most extreme (often hitting a human body), 28-37 s. The question "what's the last one?" holds
   people to the end, and the picture changes every few seconds by itself.
2. The single-power "what if you had X" Shorts over 1M come from big channels. "What if you had a" and "what if humans
   had" return almost no Shorts in search: these views come from the feed, not from search.
3. The wish -> backfire premise is proven far outside our channel (wrong superpower 170M) - keep it.
4. Every small-channel outlier sat on a ~30K median; ours is 1.4K. Two jobs: lift the floor with the single-power
   formula (shark), and take an occasional swing with the escalating format.
5. Also seen, not for us: AI "animal transformation" morph Shorts (39-116M, 12-20 s). Low-effort AI and against
   YouTube's 2026 push on "AI slop"; noted only because the transformation moment is the picture.

## Proposed test (only if the user says yes)

"Animal jumps, ranked" in our style: the guy jumps like a kangaroo, then a frog, then a flea; a doodle height meter
shoots up each time; the flea jump throws him about 25 storeys high and the landing is the backfire on his body
(tiny animals fall slowly, he doesn't - Haldane); it loops into the first jump. About 25 s, frame 0 = a jump already
in the air with a real whoosh. Weak spot: FEEL IT NOW (legs). Verify every jump number in the primary studies
(the cat flea numbers screened on 2026-11-19 are in PLAN.md, "Also screened"). Other ladders to screen: strength (needs a
verified backfire), speed (the cheetah overheating story was disproved, Hetem et al. 2013). Avoid a bite-force ladder
on a meter: too close to SciFX's video.
