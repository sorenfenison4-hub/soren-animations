# Shorts research - 2026-11-19

Why: the user said the owl and shark Shorts did well and the rest stall at 1-3K (some ~5K), and asked for a full look at
the YouTube algorithm, "view jail" and what the top Shorts do, then a diagnosis of our videos and fixes. The fixes are
now rules in CLAUDE.md (Growth: PREMISE SCREEN, SHOW THE BACKFIRE, WATCHED THE TOP SHORTS, CADENCE, DIAGNOSIS) and the
screened idea list is in PLAN.md (UP NEXT). This file keeps the evidence behind them.

How: ~30 web searches (most article sites are blocked from this environment, so only search summaries); YouTube opened
in the environment on 2026-11-19 (www.youtube.com, youtube.com, m.youtube.com, consent.youtube.com, i.ytimg.com,
youtubei.googleapis.com). From this server YouTube's player asks to sign in ("confirm you're not a bot") - not
bypassed - so only public data was used: channel Shorts lists with views, Shorts-only search, and the public frames
(`frame0.jpg` = the vertical first frame, `maxres1-3.jpg` = about 25/50/75 %). Tools and commands: research/README.md.
Our own openings: frames at 0, 1 and 3 s of every animal Short from `videos/*/renders/*1080x1920*.mp4` (ffmpeg).
Raw numbers: research/data/2026-11-19.json (the tables at the end were built with research/report_tables.py from the
tools' output; a new run of the tools gives a new dated file to compare with).

## Conclusions (each one is a rule in CLAUDE.md now)

1. Premise decides: our hits are powers people already wish for, then the power backfires on your own body (owl:
   see in the dark -> your eyes lock; shark: a killer bite -> the first bite rips your teeth out; wings 6.7K; gills
   4.6K). The stalled ones are anatomy nobody wishes for (frog eyes, woodpecker head, snake jaw, dolphin blowhole, bat
   ears, chameleon eyes, giraffe neck). Same kit, same length (18-23 s), same tempo. The same split shows on another
   channel: Posit Pixel "If A Human Had Greenland Shark Anatomy" 5.8M (live 500 years -> a parasite eats your eyes) vs
   "If A Human Had Giraffe Anatomy" 190K, their worst Short.
2. Show the backfire on his body in a close-up by ~3 s (shark: the teeth rip out into the apple at 2.6 s). Most of our
   stalled openings are the same medium shot: a small guy standing beside an animal under a text box.
3. Every big narrated what-if Short burns in word-by-word captions (Zack D. Films, Posit Pixel, CoolFacts, Malas 3D,
   FactoMatic). We never had captions - test them from the next video.
4. Their frame 0 is a close-up (a face or the strange body part fills the frame); ours is a small full-body guy.
5. A blank / black frame 0 flops (a "What If Humans Had Animal Super Senses?" Short that opens on black text: 2.2K).
6. Silent daily-life gags travel: Malas 3D (a simple round-headed 3D guy) "As Tall as an Electric Pole" 12M (head
   through the roof) while our giraffe neck explained blood pressure (3.7K).
7. Too many, too alike: 17 Shorts made in 6 days (11-13 to 11-18), often two within an hour, three "eyes" videos in
   three days -> at most one a day, no same body part or animal within 3 uploads.
8. All six Shorts whose voice ended "X or Y? Comment below." stalled (chameleon, eel/axolotl, woodpecker, frog,
   dolphin, bat) - already replaced by an on-screen ask on 11-18 (shark was the first without it).
9. Not the cause: length, voice tempo (bat and dolphin used shark's 1.12), the like/subscribe stickers and the comment
   bubble (owl and shark have them). Like rate is about the same on every video, so people who see them like them.
10. A topic is not enough on its own: "Why don't birds get electrocuted?" has 10.3M on Zack D. Films and 10.3M on
    Professor Of How; ours ("Why don't birds on power lines get shocked?") has 79. Packaging, frame 0 and the
    channel's test audience matter as much.

Caveat: two hits is a small sample. Confirm with Studio: "Viewed vs swiped away" + average % viewed for shark, owl,
octopus and two stalled Shorts. Also check the Audience setting ("made for kids" turns comments off).

## YouTube in 2026 (web research)

Said by YouTube, or reported the same way by several outlets:
- 2025-03-31: a Shorts view counts on every start and replay (no minimum); "engaged views" keep the old count.
- 2026-01-21: CEO letter - "managing AI slop" (low-quality, repetitive videos) is a priority for 2026.
- 2026-01: Shorts get their own filter in search.
- late June 2026: the dislike button left the Shorts player; "Not interested" (with reasons) and "Don't recommend this
  channel" are now the negative signals.
- 2026-08-24: long-form views also count from the first frame (Shorts already did).
- 2026-09-01: Creator Insider with Todd Beaupre (growth and discovery): no single metric decides; satisfaction surveys
  count; subscribers and new viewers are learned separately; the system keeps learning after the first hours.
- 2026-09-23: Shorts series (seasons / episodes, a "Watch series" button) for Partner Program channels.
- 2026-10-01: Shorts recommendations favour original content and cut the reach of re-uploads of other creators'
  videos; voice-over or small edits on someone else's clip do not count as original.
- 2026-11-16: estimated TV co-viewers added to public view counts (TV only).
- 2023, Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead): each Short is tested on a seed audience; posting more often does not change
  how one Short does; most views come from swiping, so few people see a Short's thumbnail.

Creator / analyst claims (not confirmed by YouTube - treat as directional):
- "View jail" is not an official thing; a Short that stalls lost its test audience.
- "Viewed vs swiped away" targets of 70-80 % come from creator reports; YouTube publishes no benchmarks.
- Shorts older than ~28-30 days get little new push (analysts, from a few channels' graphs).
- Posting several Shorts a day raises total channel views with no consistent drop per Short (a 16,815-video study);
  "one a day, 8-12 h apart" advice has no public data behind it.
- Shares / sends probably weigh more than likes.
- Hooks: visual, clear on mute, under ~3 s, foreshadow the payoff, end on a loop (Shorts strategists such as Jenny Hoyos).
