Title: What if you could turn into a butterfly?

What happens inside a chrysalis?

Inside the chrysalis, a caterpillar digests itself. Enzymes break down most of its body into a kind of soup. A few groups of cells survive, called imaginal discs, and each one builds a part of the butterfly: the wings, the legs, the eyes.

A monarch spends about 8 to 15 days as a chrysalis, most often 10 to 14, and less when it is warm.

A grown butterfly has no working jaws, so it can never chew again. It drinks through a long tube called a proboscis, which it keeps rolled up like a spring. When it first comes out, that tube is in two halves, and it has to join them together before it can drink.
