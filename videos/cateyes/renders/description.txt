TITLE
What if you had a cat's eyes?

DESCRIPTION
Can cats see in the dark?

Almost. A cat can't see in total darkness, but it sees far better than you in very dim light. Behind the retina of each eye is a mirror layer. Light that passes the retina bounces off it and goes through a second time, so the eye gets two chances to catch it. That mirror is also why cat eyes glow in photos taken with a flash.

The cost is detail. A cat's eye is built for the dark, with mostly night cells and far fewer of the cells for colour and fine detail. By day, a cat sees about seven times less detail than you: the big E at the top of an eye chart, and not much below it.

One more trick: a cat's slit pupil can change its area about 135 times, from a thin line in sunlight to a wide circle at night. A round human pupil changes only about 15 times.
