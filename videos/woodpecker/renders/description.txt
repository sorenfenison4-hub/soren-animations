TITLE: What if you had a woodpecker's head?

How do woodpeckers not get a concussion?

A woodpecker can hit a tree up to twenty times a second. For a long time people thought its skull worked like a shock absorber. High-speed video and computer models of three woodpecker species showed the opposite: the head works like a stiff hammer, because any cushioning would waste the force of the peck.

Each of those hits slows the head down harder than what gives a person or a monkey a concussion. The woodpecker gets away with it because its brain is tiny. A small brain feels much less pressure from the same jolt than a big human brain does.

Bonus fact: a woodpecker's tongue is so long that it wraps around the back of its skull.

Please don't try headbutting a tree. Hits to the head can cause concussions even when they seem small.
