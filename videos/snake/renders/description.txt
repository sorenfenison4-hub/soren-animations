TITLE
What if you could swallow like a snake?

DESCRIPTION
How do snakes swallow such big things?

A snake's jaw doesn't unhinge. The two halves of its lower jaw aren't joined at the chin, so stretchy tissue lets them spread far apart. In big Burmese pythons, the mouth can open about 26 centimeters wide. Yours opens about 5.

While it swallows, a snake slides the opening of its windpipe forward along the floor of its mouth, past the food, like a snorkel. That lets it keep breathing during a swallow that can take a long time.

You can't do that. Every time you swallow, a flap closes over your windpipe and your breathing stops for a moment. A piece of food stuck in your throat can block your airway, so always take bites you can chew.
