TITLE: What if you had a shark's teeth?

Why do sharks never run out of teeth?

A shark's teeth are not locked into bone the way yours are. They sit in the gum on top of the jaw, so they come loose and fall out all the time, often while the shark is eating.

That's fine for a shark. Behind the front teeth, rows of spare teeth are already growing, folded back inside the gum, and they move forward like a conveyor belt to fill every gap. In young lemon sharks a whole new row moves into place about every two weeks, and they keep doing it for life.

You get two sets of teeth: baby teeth and adult teeth. Your adult teeth have roots set deep in your jawbone, and a lost one never grows back, so protect them in sports and see a dentist fast if one gets knocked out.
