TITLE: What if you had a turtle's shell?

How do turtles breathe inside their shell?

A turtle's shell is not something it wears. It is part of its skeleton: more than 50 bones, including its backbone and its ribs, joined into one hard case. That is also why a turtle can never climb out of its shell.

Your ribs work differently. Every time you breathe in, the muscles between them lift your ribs up and out while your diaphragm pulls down, and your lungs fill. With your ribs locked inside a shell, your chest could not rise, so you could never take a deep breath.

Turtles solved this a long time ago. Instead of moving their ribs, they breathe with a sling of muscles inside the shell that pulls and pushes around their lungs. Fossils suggest turtle ancestors were already breathing this way about 260 million years ago, long before the full shell existed.
