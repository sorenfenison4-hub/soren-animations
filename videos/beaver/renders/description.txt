What if you had a beaver's teeth?

DESCRIPTION
Why do beavers' teeth never stop growing?

A beaver's front teeth keep growing for its whole life. The front of each tooth is hard orange enamel with iron in it, and the softer back wears away faster, so every bite keeps the edge sharp. Scientists once timed a beaver cutting through a branch more than 6 centimetres thick in under 30 seconds.

The catch is that a beaver has to gnaw every day to wear its teeth down. If it can't, they keep growing until its mouth can't close properly. Captive beavers that had nothing to gnaw on grew teeth into the opposite jaw and could no longer eat.

Your adult teeth stop growing once they are fully formed, so you never have to chew wood to keep them short.
