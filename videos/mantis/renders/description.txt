Title: What if you had a mantis shrimp's punch?

How does a mantis shrimp punch so hard?

The peacock mantis shrimp swings a hammer-shaped club at up to about 80 km an hour, faster than you can blink. It uses a built-in spring that it loads up and then lets go, which is how it breaks open snail shells.

The club moves so fast that the water behind it can't keep up. Bubbles form in that gap and then collapse, and the collapse hits the shell a second time.

A human fist moving that fast would crack on the first hit. The shrimp's club is built from layers stacked in a spiral, so cracks twist and stop instead of running straight through. It also grows a fresh club every time it molts.
