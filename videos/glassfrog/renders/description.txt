TITLE
What if you could turn see-through like a glass frog?

DESCRIPTION
How do glass frogs turn see-through?

Glass frogs sleep through the day on leaves. While they rest, they pull about nine in ten of their red blood cells out of their blood and pack them into their liver, which is covered in tiny mirror-like crystals. That makes them two to three times more see-through. When they wake up, the cells go back into the blood.

Your red blood cells carry oxygen to your brain. If nine in ten of them left your blood, your brain would run short of oxygen and you would pass out. Packing that many cells together would also normally cause blood clots. Nobody knows yet how glass frogs avoid them.

Press a fingernail and let go: it turns white, then pink again. That pink is your blood.
