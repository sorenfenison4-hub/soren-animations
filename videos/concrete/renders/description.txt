TITLE
Why has Roman concrete lasted 2,000 years?

DESCRIPTION
Roman concrete can heal its own cracks. The Romans mixed it with quicklime, which gets hot as it reacts and leaves little white lumps of lime all through the concrete.

When water gets into a crack, the lime in those lumps dissolves, then hardens again as new stone and seals the crack. There's also no steel inside, so nothing can rust, swell and crack it from the inside, which is how a lot of modern concrete breaks down.

The Pantheon in Rome, finished about 1,900 years ago, still has the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world.

Quicklime is caustic and gets very hot when it touches water, so don't try mixing it yourself.
