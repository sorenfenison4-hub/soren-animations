TITLE: What if you had a frog's eyes?

Why do frogs blink when they swallow?

A frog doesn't just blink when it eats. It pulls its eyes down into its head, and they bulge into the roof of its mouth. The eyes press on the food and help push it toward the throat. In a study of leopard frogs, frogs that couldn't pull their eyes in needed about 4 swallows per cricket instead of 2.3.

Catching the food is even faster. A frog's tongue can grab an insect in under 0.07 seconds, about five times faster than you blink. The tongue is also about ten times softer than yours, so it wraps around the bug like a sticky glove.

Your eyes don't work that way. Pressing on your eyeballs can set off a reflex that slows your heart, so don't push on your eyes.
