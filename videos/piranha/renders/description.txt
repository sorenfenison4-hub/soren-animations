What if you had a piranha's teeth?

DESCRIPTION
How do piranhas replace their teeth?

A piranha's teeth lock into each other along the jaw, so each row works like one saw blade. And they don't fall out one by one. Every tooth on one side of the mouth comes out at once, and a new set that was already growing underneath takes its place. Later, the other side does the same.

That bite is strong. In one study a black piranha of about 1 kilo bit with more than 320 newtons, about 30 times its own weight and the strongest bite measured for any bony fish. For a person, 30 times your weight would be harder than the strongest crocodile bite ever measured.

So with piranha teeth you could bite through almost anything, but half your smile would drop out at a time.
