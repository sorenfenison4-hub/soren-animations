TITLE
Why is the Moon drifting away from Earth?

DESCRIPTION
The Moon is slowly leaving us, about 3.8 centimeters a year. Scientists measure it by bouncing lasers off mirrors the Apollo astronauts left on the Moon.

The Moon's gravity pulls Earth's oceans into a bulge. Earth spins faster than the Moon goes around it, so the bulge gets carried a little ahead of the Moon and tugs it forward. That extra pull slings the Moon into a slightly bigger orbit, about as fast as your fingernails grow.

The same tug slows Earth's spin a tiny bit, so our days are slowly getting longer.
