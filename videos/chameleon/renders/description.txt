TITLE
What if you had a chameleon's eyes?

DESCRIPTION
Can a chameleon move its eyes separately?

Yes. Each chameleon eye sits in a cone-shaped turret and swivels on its own, so one eye can look forward while the other looks behind. Together they see nearly all the way around, about 360 degrees. You see about 200. Scientists recently found that a chameleon's optic nerves are coiled like a phone cord, which gives each eye the slack to spin without damage.

Your brain is built to merge the pictures from both eyes into one. If your eyes pointed two different ways, the two pictures wouldn't match, and you'd see double. When it finds food, a chameleon points both eyes forward to aim its tongue.

Sudden double vision in real life can be a sign of something serious, so get it checked by a doctor right away.
